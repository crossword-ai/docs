title: "Proactive message"
description: "Messages Crossword sends before the shopper asks"

A proactive message is one Crossword sends without waiting for the shopper to type. It appears as a nudge from the widget while the shopper is on a page: a sizing offer after fifteen seconds of silence, or "Still deciding between these?" after they open two product pages. Per experience, you choose whether only the highest-priority match shows or every match is queued in priority order.

## What it contains

| Property | What it holds |
| -------- | ------------- |
| Content | The text and media the message carries |
| Embedded components | Any card or interactive component from the [Component library](/components/overview), plus an optional suggestion set |
| Presentation | How the message appears on the surface, such as a widget bubble or an expanded panel |
| Rule | The conditions, timing, priority, and delivery restrictions that fire it from a Journey |
| Dismiss behavior | What happens when the shopper closes the message, and whether it can return |

The text and media belong to the proactive message. The cards it embeds are plain components with no rule of their own. They show because the message that carries them was delivered.

A proactive message answers one of two questions, and the answer decides where it lives. **Contextual** asks what should happen right now, given this session. On a product page, fifteen seconds idle, show sizing help. That belongs in a Journey, as a component with a rule. **Lifecycle** asks what should happen to this person over the next hours or days. Cart abandoned, wait 30 minutes, send a message, wait 6 hours, check for purchase. That belongs in a Campaign. A Campaign step can deliver a proactive message directly. The message then carries a Campaign reference instead of a rule, and the Campaign's own delivery limits and suppression govern it. Read [Journeys vs campaigns](/orchestration/journeys-vs-campaigns#proactive-messages-which-one) for the full test.

## Rule

Because it interrupts, a proactive message carries the full rule: conditions, timing, priority, and delivery restrictions.

| Rule field | What you can set |
| ---------- | ---------------- |
| Conditions | UTM parameters, referrer or arrival source, page or URL, segment membership, shopper attributes, session behavior (scroll depth, cart contents, previous interaction), device and browser |
| Condition combinations | All, any, and exclusions |
| Timing | Delay after the page loads, elapsed time on the page, inactivity, or a scheduled window |
| Priority | Orders candidates in both delivery modes |
| Delivery restrictions | Frequency cap, suppression after dismissal or after another message, and repeat behavior across sessions |

Timing and conditions combine. A rule can require that the shopper is on a product page **and** has been idle for fifteen seconds **and** has nothing in the cart.

The proactive message Rulebook resolves candidates in one of two modes.

| Mode | Behavior |
| ---- | -------- |
| First by priority | Show only the highest-priority message whose rule and timing match |
| Stack | Queue every matching message and deliver them in priority order |

In both modes each message's own delivery restrictions still apply. A message that has hit its frequency cap is skipped even if it wins on priority.

Read [How rulebooks resolve conflicts](/orchestration/how-rulebooks-resolve#proactive-messages) for how the two modes interact with caps.

## Examples

The same sneaker storefront with the widget closed, first with no rule firing and then with one rule at a time deciding what rises from the launcher and when.

### Out of the box

A shopper browses a product page with the widget closed. Nothing rises from the launcher in the bottom corner, however long they stay, because the Default Journey on most storefronts carries no proactive message. This is what every shopper sees when nothing else matches. Silence is the out-of-the-box result for this component, and it is a valid one.

### With a rule: inactivity on a product page

> Rule: UTM campaign is unboxing-a and page type is product. Timing: 15 seconds of inactivity. Priority 10. Once per session.

A shopper from the brand's unboxing campaign has been on a product page for fifteen seconds without scrolling, tapping, or opening the widget. A bubble rises from the closed launcher: "Need help finding your size?" with two chips beneath it, "Yes, help me" and "No thanks", and a close control in its corner. The rest of the page is unchanged. In First by priority mode only this message shows, even though a lower-priority shipping nudge also matched. In Stack mode the shipping nudge would queue and appear once the shopper dismisses this one.

### With a rule: scroll depth on a collection page

> Rule: path starts with /collections/ and scroll depth is over 60%. Priority 8. Once per session.

A shopper scrolls most of the way down the running collection. As they pass the 60% mark, a bubble rises: "Seeing a lot of options? Tell me what you run on and I'll narrow it down", with one chip, "Narrow it down". This one fires on something the shopper did rather than on something they did not do, so there is no timing on the rule at all. The condition alone decides the moment.

### With a rule: cart value with a frequency cap

> Rule: cart value is over $75 and under $100. Timing: 30 seconds elapsed on the page. Priority 12. Frequency cap once per week. If dismissed, do not show again for 14 days.

A shopper with one pair in the cart has been on the site for half a minute. A bubble rises: "You're a few dollars from free shipping. Want to see socks that get you there?" with a product collection of three socks embedded beneath it. The shopper saw this once already this week, so on their next visit the Rulebook skips it even though it still matches and outranks the sizing nudge. The cap, not the priority, decides whether it shows.

### With a rule: sale weekend

> Rule: any page. Timing: scheduled window from Friday 00:00 to Sunday 23:59 of the sale weekend. Exclusion: member of "Opened chat this session". Priority 5. Once per session.

During the sale weekend a shopper lands on the home page. Ten seconds in, a bubble rises: "Sale ends Sunday. Want me to find the best deal in your size?" with a "Find my deal" chip. Outside the window the rule never becomes a candidate, so nothing is needed to switch it off on Monday. The exclusion keeps it from interrupting a shopper who has already opened chat. In Stack mode, a shopper who also triggers the sizing nudge sees the sizing nudge first and this one after they dismiss it.

### What else a rule can do

- Fire on a referrer, such as a visitor from a social network with no UTM parameters.
- Change presentation by device, such as a bottom sheet on mobile and a bubble on desktop.
- Respond to previous interaction, such as "Still deciding?" when the shopper returns to the same product page twice in a session.
- Wait for elapsed time in the session rather than on one page.
- Exclude a segment, such as never nudging members of "Opted out of promotions".
- Suppress itself after another proactive message has already been delivered this session.
- Set repeat behavior after the shopper acts on it, such as never showing again once they tap a chip.
- Embed a quiz or a form so the nudge collects an answer instead of opening chat.

Every condition and how they combine is in [Rules](/orchestration/rules).
