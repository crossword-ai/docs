A comparison section places a comparison on a merchant page. Two or more products sit side by side across the attributes that matter, with the differences explained, so a shopper who is choosing between options can decide without opening the chat. It brings the comparison card out of the conversation and onto the page where the decision is being made.

## Examples

Every scene below is a comparison section on a sneaker brand's product page, first with no rule and then with a different rule each time.

### Out of the box

The Default Playbook's section sits on the product page, inserted below the description, for every shopper. The heading reads **How it compares**, and the table shows the pair on the page against its two nearest alternatives as columns, with **Cushioning**, **Weight**, and **Price** as rows. No product is marked as recommended. Under the table, a single button reads **Help me choose**, and tapping it opens the widget. No conditions, priority 0.

### With a rule: arrival from a campaign

> Rule: product page, related-products carousel, replace. UTM campaign is `unboxing-a`. Priority 10.

A shopper who follows the link from an unboxing video sees the storefront's "You may also like" carousel replaced by a comparison. The intro reads "The pair from the video against its two closest alternatives", the featured pair is marked **Recommended for daily miles**, and two buttons read **Choose this pair** and **Show me a third option**. The default section below the description is untouched, because it is a different placement.

### With a rule: two products viewed in a row

> Rule: product page, above the add-to-cart button, insert. Session behavior: viewed two products in a row. Priority 20.

A shopper who has just moved from one product page to another sees a two-column comparison right above the add-to-cart button: this pair against the one they looked at a moment ago. The intro reads "Still deciding? Here is how these two compare.", and the button reads **Go back to the other pair**. A shopper who arrives on the page cold never sees it.

### With a rule: marathon runners

> Rule: product page, below the description, insert. Member of "Marathon runners". Priority 15.

A shopper in the marathon segment sees the section in the same spot as the default, but the rows are different: **Cushioning**, **Drop**, and **Race-day weight**, with the pair on the page marked **Recommended for race day**. At priority 15 this section outranks the default at 0 for the same placement, so the marathon runner sees one comparison, not two.

### With a rule: product out of stock

> Rule: product page, below the sold-out notice, insert. Product on the page is out of stock. Priority 30.

On a sold-out pair, a comparison appears under the storefront's "Sold out" notice: the sold-out pair against two in-stock alternatives, with the closest marked **Closest match, in stock**. The button under the table reads **Choose the closest match**, and tapping it opens the widget with that pair already selected.

### What else a rule can do

- Compare the top three in a collection, above the grid, for shoppers arriving from a search ad.
- Show a two-column comparison on mobile and a three-column one on desktop.
- Show the section only during a scheduled window, such as a launch week.
- Delay the section until the shopper has scrolled past the gallery.
- Exclude shoppers who already have the pair in their cart.
- Draw the rows from the attribute the shopper asked about in chat.
- Cap the section to once per session so it does not follow the shopper between products.
- Match any of several referrers with one rule.

Every condition, combination, timing option, and delivery limit is listed in [Rules](/orchestration/rules). The shared rule shape is under [Page sections](/components/page/page-sections).

## Anatomy

| Property | What it holds |
| -------- | ------------- |
| Heading | The title above the comparison, such as **How it compares**. |
| Introduction | Optional copy that frames the comparison, such as which products were chosen and why. Supports dynamic variables. |
| Comparison card | The embedded [comparison card](/components/cards/comparison-card): the items, the attributes, the values with their explanations, an optional recommendation, and the selection or next-step controls. |

The items can be fixed, such as the product on the page against its two nearest alternatives, or chosen at display time from what Crossword knows about the shopper. The attributes can be fixed for a category or drawn from what the shopper asked about in chat.

## Where it appears

- **Product page.** The product on the page against its alternatives. The most common use.
- **Collection page.** The top picks in the collection side by side, above or below the grid.
- **Dynamic landing page.** The featured product against alternatives, in one of the layout's placements.

The section goes into a placement. It can be inserted at a location or replace an existing part of the page, such as a related-products carousel. Read [Pages and placements](/orchestration/pages-and-placements).

## Rules

A comparison section carries its own rule, scoped by its target page and placement. Target page, placement, and placement action are required. Conditions, priority, timing, and delivery limits are optional.

The rule decides whether the section shows. The controls on the embedded card act once it is on screen, and a tap on **Choose Product A** or **Show me a third option** can open the conversation.
