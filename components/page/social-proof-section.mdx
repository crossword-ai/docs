title: "Social proof section"
description: "The social proof section shows reviews, testimonials, and ratings picked for each shopper's context, such as reviews from runners for a marathon shopper."
---

A social proof section shows reviews, testimonials, or data such as ratings and purchase counts, selected for the shopper's context. A marathon runner sees reviews that mention marathons. A shopper who asked about wide feet sees reviews that mention fit. It answers the question "do people like me like this?" with evidence rather than copy.

## Anatomy

| Property | What it holds |
| -------- | ------------- |
| Heading | The title of the section, such as **What runners say**. Supports dynamic variables. |
| Content | The reviews, testimonials, or data points the section draws from. Data points include average rating, review count, and purchase count. |
| Selection rules | Which items to show for this shopper: reviews that mention a use case they described, a size they asked about, or a topic tied to their segment. A minimum rating or a recency limit can narrow the set further. |
| Layout | A carousel, a list, or a single highlighted quote with a rating summary. |
| Call to action | Optional. A button or link, such as **Read all reviews**. |

## Where it appears

- **Product page.** Replace the storefront's reviews block with reviews chosen for this shopper, or insert a short summary near the add-to-cart button.
- **Collection page.** A rating summary or a highlighted testimonial above the grid.
- **Home page and dynamic landing pages.** Testimonials that match the campaign a visitor arrived from.

The section goes into a placement. Replace is the common action on product pages, since most storefronts already have a reviews block. Insert works anywhere else. Read [Pages and placements](/orchestration/pages-and-placements).

## Rules

A social proof section carries its own rule, scoped by its target page and placement. Target page, placement, and placement action are required. Conditions, priority, timing, and delivery limits are optional.

Keep the two kinds of rules apart. The rule on the section decides whether this shopper sees it. The selection rules inside the section decide which reviews they see.

## Examples

Every scene below is a social proof section on a sneaker brand's storefront. The rule on the section decides who sees it and where; the selection rules inside it decide which reviews they see.

### Out of the box

The default section sits on the product page and replaces the storefront's reviews block, for every shopper. The heading reads **What runners say**, a rating summary reads **4.6 of 5 from 1,240 reviews**, and beneath it are the three most recent reviews rated four stars or higher, in a list. A **Read all reviews** link sits under the third. No conditions, priority 0.

### With a rule: asked about fit in chat

> Rule: product page, reviews block, replace. Session behavior: raised the topic of fit in chat. Priority 10.

A shopper who asked the widget "Do these run narrow?" and then scrolls down sees the same placement, but the heading now reads **What runners say about fit** and the three reviews each mention width or sizing, with the matching phrase highlighted, such as "true to size, roomy toe box". Priority 10 outranks the default at 0 for the same placement, so this shopper sees fit reviews and nothing else.

### With a rule: marathon runners

> Rule: product page, reviews block, replace. Member of "Marathon runners". Priority 15.

A shopper in the marathon segment sees the heading **What marathoners say**, a data point reading **Worn in 312 marathons this year**, and a carousel of reviews that mention a race. The layout is a carousel rather than a list, because that is how this section is configured. Shoppers outside the segment fall through to the fit section or the default.

### With a rule: arrival from a review site

> Rule: home page, below the hero, insert. Referrer is a review site. Priority 10.

A visitor who arrives from a review site lands on the home page and sees a section inserted under the hero: a single highlighted quote, a rating summary reading **4.7 of 5 across 12,000 reviews**, and a **See what runners say** link. The home page has no reviews block of its own, so this is an insert rather than a replace. Visitors from other sources see the home page as it ships.

### With a rule: high-value cart

> Rule: cart page, above the checkout button, insert. Cart value is over $150. Priority 20.

A shopper whose cart totals more than 150 sees a compact section above the checkout button: a rating summary reading **4.7 average across the items in your cart** and one short quote about durability. It is there to steady a larger purchase at the last step. A cart under $150 does not match, and the checkout button stands alone.

### What else a rule can do

- Show a single quote on mobile and the full list on desktop.
- Match reviews to the use case the shopper described, such as trail or gym.
- Show a limited-time data point, such as this weekend's purchase count, in a scheduled window.
- Wait until the shopper has scrolled to the reviews before the section appears.
- Exclude shoppers who have already reviewed the product.
- Show campaign-specific testimonials to any of several UTM campaigns.
- Cap a cart-page section to once per session.
- Target every page under `/collections/` with a rating summary above the grid.

Every condition, combination, timing option, and delivery limit is listed in [Rules](/orchestration/rules). The shared rule shape is under [Page sections](/components/page/page-sections).
