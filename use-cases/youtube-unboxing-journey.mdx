---
title: "YouTube unboxing journey"
description: "Greet shoppers arriving from a video and sell them the pair they just watched"
---

A sneaker brand sends Product A to a creator, and the creator publishes an unboxing video with a link to the product page in the description. Shoppers who click that link are not browsing. They have watched the box open, they know the colorway, and they arrive with one question: is this the pair from the video, and does it come in my size? The merchant goal is to convert those shoppers into add-to-carts by answering that question before they have to ask it. One Journey does this. Apart from one starter on Product A's page, it changes nothing for the rest of your traffic.

## What the shopper sees

The screenshot below is Product A's page as the shopper sees it about 15 seconds after arriving from the video. The widget is still closed. Above the launcher, the "Looking for the pair from the video?" greeting sits over the **Does this pair run true to size?** starter and two Default starters, the "As seen in the unboxing" content block sits below the buy box, and the "Need help finding your size?" nudge has just appeared. One frame holds every component the Journey places for an in-stock pair.

A shopper finishes the video, clicks the link, and lands on Product A's page. The URL carries `utm_source=youtube`, `utm_campaign=unboxing-a`, and `utm_content=product-a`.

1. **Arrival greeting.** A second after the page loads, a proactive message rises from the launcher: "Looking for the pair from the video?" This is the part that knows the shopper came from the video. The widget's welcome message does not change. It is global, so this shopper sees the storefront's usual "Hello! How can I help?" when they open the widget, like everyone else.
2. **Starters.** Three conversation starters stack above the launcher and sit under the welcome message once the widget opens. The first is **Does this pair run true to size?**, set on Product A's page. The other two come from the Default Journey. Starters depend only on the page, so every shopper on Product A's page sees the same three, whether they came from the video or not.
3. **Content block.** Below the buy box, a content block titled "As seen in the unboxing" shows a still from the video, the colorway the creator unboxed, and the size they wore.
4. **Proactive nudge.** The shopper scrolls for 15 seconds without opening the widget or clicking a starter. A proactive message appears: "Need help finding your size?"
5. **Out of stock.** If the featured SKU is sold out, the arrival greeting reads "The pair from the video is sold out. Want the closest alternatives?" instead, and a recommendation section titled "Close to the pair from the video" replaces the sold-out notice with three alternatives. The starters stay the same.

Every one of these decisions is made fresh on each page load from the current session. Nothing is remembered from a previous visit and nothing waits for tomorrow. That is why this is a [Journey and not a Campaign](/orchestration/journeys-vs-campaigns).

## Building blocks used

One Journey, YouTube Unboxing, holds six components. Each one carries its own [rule](/orchestration/rules). The Journey has no rule of its own.

| Component | Rule | Priority | What it shows |
| --------- | ---- | -------- | ------------- |
| [Proactive message](/components/conversation/proactive-message) | UTM source is `youtube`, UTM campaign is `unboxing-a`, and the product in `utm_content` is in stock, 1 second after the page loads | 10 | Greeting text: "Looking for the pair from the video?" |
| [Proactive message](/components/conversation/proactive-message) | UTM source is `youtube`, UTM campaign is `unboxing-a`, and the product in `utm_content` is out of stock, 1 second after the page loads | 10 | Greeting text: "The pair from the video is sold out. Want the closest alternatives?" |
| [Conversation starter](/components/conversation/conversation-starter) | Page is Product A's page | 10 | Starter label: **Does this pair run true to size?** |
| [Content block](/components/page/content-block) | Page is a product page and UTM source is `youtube` | 10 | "As seen in the unboxing": a still from the video, the colorway, and the size the creator wore, below the buy box |
| [Proactive message](/components/conversation/proactive-message) | Page is a product page, UTM source is `youtube`, and 15 seconds of inactivity have elapsed | 10 | Message text: "Need help finding your size?" |
| [Recommendation section](/components/page/recommendation-section) | Page is a product page, UTM source is `youtube`, and the product in `utm_content` is out of stock | 10 | "Close to the pair from the video": three alternatives in place of the sold-out notice |

The two arrival greetings carry opposite stock conditions, so only one of them is ever a candidate for a given shopper. The same is true of the content block and the recommendation section. The starter carries only a page rule, so it does not test the arrival source or stock. Everything that depends on arriving from the video is a proactive message or a page section.

Three [Rulebooks](/orchestration/rulebooks) resolve these candidates against everything else that is enabled. The welcome message is not one of them. It carries no rule, so every shopper sees the Default Journey's welcome message whether they came from the video or not.

- **Conversation starter selection** returns a set for the page. With the Rulebook set to take 1 from the highest-priority rule and then 1 from each remaining rule, up to a total of 3, every shopper on Product A's page sees this Journey's starter first and two Default starters after it.
- **Proactive message delivery** checks the timing on each candidate. The arrival greeting becomes a candidate one second after the page loads, and at priority 10 it wins over any lower-priority greeting from another Journey. The sizing nudge only becomes a candidate once 15 seconds of inactivity have elapsed.
- **Component selection by page placement** decides which [page section](/components/page/page-sections) goes into each placement on the product page. The content block goes below the buy box. The recommendation section replaces the sold-out notice.

Two more objects take part without belonging to this Journey. The [Shopping Agent](/agents/overview) answers whatever the shopper types after clicking a starter. The [Default Journey](/orchestration/journeys) supplies the welcome message every shopper sees, the fallback starters on every page with none of its own, and the remaining two starters on Product A's page. See [How rulebooks resolve conflicts](/orchestration/how-rulebooks-resolve) for the full walk through priority and limits.

## Step by step

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the Journey">
    Open your web experience and go to **Journeys**. Click **New Journey**. Name it `YouTube Unboxing` and give it a description such as "Shoppers arriving from creator unboxing videos". Leave it disabled while you add components. A disabled Journey contributes nothing to any Rulebook, so you can build it without affecting live traffic.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the arrival greeting">
    Click **Add component** and choose **Proactive message**. Set the rule to match **all** of these conditions: **UTM source** equals `youtube`, **UTM campaign** equals `unboxing-a`, and the product referenced by **UTM content** is **in stock**. Under **Timing**, choose **Delay** and enter `1` second. Set **Priority** to `10`. Under **Delivery restrictions**, set the frequency cap to once per session. Write "Looking for the pair from the video?" as the text. Leave the welcome message alone. It is global and set in the Default Journey, so a greeting for video arrivals belongs in a proactive message.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the sold-out greeting">
    Add a second **Proactive message**. Use the same conditions, timing, priority, and frequency cap, but set the product referenced by **UTM content** to **out of stock**. Write "The pair from the video is sold out. Want the closest alternatives?" as the text. Because the two rules are mutually exclusive, the shopper never sees both.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the starter for Product A's page">
    Click **Add component** and choose **Conversation starter**. Set the rule's **Page** to Product A's page, by its path. A starter's rule has no other condition. Set **Priority** to `10`. Set the label to **Does this pair run true to size?** and the prompt to "Does this pair run true to size, and which size should I get?" Clicking the starter sends that prompt to the Shopping Agent. Every shopper on Product A's page sees this starter, so write it for anyone looking at the pair, not only video arrivals.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the content block">
    Click **Add component** and choose **Content block**. Set **Target page** to **Product** and **Placement** to the location below the buy box, with mode **Insert**. Set the rule to **UTM source** equals `youtube` and **Priority** to `10`. Title the block "As seen in the unboxing". Give it a media [primitive](/components/primitives) for the video still, a text primitive for the colorway and the size the creator wore, and a button primitive labelled **Add to cart** that adds the featured SKU.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the proactive sizing nudge">
    Click **Add component** and choose **Proactive message**. Set the conditions to **Page type** is **Product** and **UTM source** equals `youtube`. Under **Timing**, choose **Inactivity** and enter `15` seconds. Inactivity means no widget open, no starter click, and no message sent. Set **Priority** to `10`. Under **Delivery restrictions**, set the frequency cap to once per session so a shopper who dismisses it does not see it again on the next product page. Write "Need help finding your size?" as the text and add a quick reply set of common sizes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the alternatives for a sold-out SKU">
    Click **Add component** and choose **Recommendation section**. Set **Target page** to **Product** and **Placement** to the sold-out notice, with mode **Replace**. Set the rule to **UTM source** equals `youtube` and the product referenced by **UTM content** is **out of stock**. Set **Priority** to `10`. Title the section "Close to the pair from the video". Configure its [product collection](/components/cards/product-collection) to return three products from the same category and colorway family as the featured SKU, each rendered as a [product card](/components/cards/product-card) with its own **Add to cart** button.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable the Journey alongside the Default">
    Return to the Journey and switch **Enabled** on. Do not disable the Default Journey or any other Journey. Enabling YouTube Unboxing makes its six rules eligible. It does not switch anything on for shoppers whose session does not match. Returning Customer, VIP, and any other Journey you run keep contributing their own candidates, and the Rulebooks resolve them together.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the result on the product page">
    Go to **Deployment** and publish the experience to your **Test** environment. Crossword assigns the revision a number. On a storefront that runs the Test installation snippet, open Product A's page with the video's query string appended: `?utm_source=youtube&utm_campaign=unboxing-a&utm_content=product-a`. Confirm the arrival greeting, the first starter, and the content block. Wait 15 seconds without touching the widget and confirm the sizing nudge. Then open the same page without the query string. Confirm there is no arrival greeting and no content block, and that the starters are the same as before, because they depend only on the page. Open the widget in both cases and confirm the welcome message is the same. If Product A has a sold-out variant, set `utm_content` to that variant and confirm the sold-out greeting and the alternatives. When everything matches, publish to **Production**. See [Deployment](/orchestration/deployment) for environments and revisions.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Measuring it

The goal event is `add_to_cart`. Each component the shopper sees has its own identity and emits its own impression and interaction events, so you can follow one shopper from the first thing they saw to the cart.

| Signal | What you count | What it tells you |
| ------ | -------------- | ----------------- |
| Greeting impressions | Sessions where the YouTube Unboxing arrival greeting or sold-out greeting was delivered | How many video arrivals the rule caught. If this is far below the click count in your video analytics, check the UTM parameters in the link. |
| Greeting engagement | Replies to either greeting, divided by greeting impressions | Whether the greeting matches what video arrivals came for |
| Starter clicks | Clicks on **Does this pair run true to size?** in sessions that saw the arrival greeting, compared with sessions on Product A's page that did not | Whether the page's starter helps video arrivals as much as everyone else |
| Proactive message engagement | Sizing nudges shown, and how many led to a reply, a quick-reply tap, or a dismiss | Whether 15 seconds is the right delay and whether sizing is the right question |
| Add to cart | `add_to_cart` events in sessions that saw the YouTube Unboxing arrival greeting, divided by greeting impressions | The conversion rate for video traffic |

Compare the add-to-cart rate for sessions that saw this Journey's arrival greeting with the rate for the same UTM source before you enabled it. For a controlled read, run an [Experiment](/orchestration/experiments) between this Journey and a copy with the proactive nudge removed. The [discovery experiment](/use-cases/discovery-experiment) walkthrough shows how.
